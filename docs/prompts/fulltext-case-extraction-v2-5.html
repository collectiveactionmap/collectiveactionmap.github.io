OSTARA / MODEL INSTRUCTIONS

W09 · Full-text case data extraction

Case-level extraction instructions and eligibility criteria.

Purpose

Identify distinct cases of collective action in forest-risk commodities relating to sustainable supply chains, sustainable production, and/or sustainable production areas. For each eligible case, extract the information specified in the accompanying case-analysis CSV. Extract what the source reports about progress, success, failure and impacts where the CSV requests it; do not independently evaluate effectiveness, infer causal impacts, or conduct new statistical analyses.

Case Definition And Boundaries

A case is synonymous to a collective-action initiative: a distinguishable real-world programme or project bringing multiple actors together to collaboratively address shared problems and/or achieve shared objectives. A case has an identifiable implementation context, such as a place or region, supply chain or commodity, country or market, or policy setting. The source need not use the term “collective action” if the initiative meets this definition.

Do not mistake a research project, single commercial service, generic recommendation, or named location for an eligible case. Extract every distinct initiative supported by the full text, including different initiatives undertaken by the same coalition. Do not merge initiatives merely because they share a coalition; do not split an initiative merely because it has different names; and do not merge genuinely distinct initiatives because they overlap in actors, contracts or geography. Explain genuinely uncertain case boundaries in assessment_reason. Do not invent an initiative from background context or aspirations alone.

For a case of collective action to qualify, it must meet ALL of the following mandatory criteria:

Commodity Supply Chains

The case must relate to collective action to achieve improvements directly or indirectly in the sustainability supply chains and/or the production and/or the production areas within forest risk commodities identified as: soy, palm oil, cocoa/cacao, coffee, timber, beef, rubber, sugarcane, rice, cassava, maize, pulp and paper.

Objective

The case must relate to collective action that aims to achieve commonly agreed environmental and/or social sustainability objectives concerning deforestation, ecosystem state, biodiversity, resilience, livelihoods, food security, resource management, equity, human rights, human wellbeing, whether to achieve any of the three: sustainability compliance and/or sustainability commitments and/or deliver positive impact on the ground. An aim to achieve economic sustainability alone without an objective for environmental and/or social sustainability is not sufficient. Explicit use of “sustainability” is not required but the objective of the collective action must be relevant to an environmental and/or social sustainability agenda.

Substantive System Focus

The case must relate to collective action that occurs: (a) within / across supply chains AND/OR within / across production areas AND/OR within / across producer country systems; AND (b) directly or indirectly linked to supply chain sustainability.

Scale

The case must relate to collective action that operates beyond the scale of a project by an independent actor or a single private property. Place-based, landscape-based, sourcing area-based, production area-based, supply shed-based, ecosystem-level, jurisdictional, regional, national, international and sectoral arrangements can qualify.

Authoritative Case-Analysis Csv

The supplied CSV defines the extraction fields, their definitions, topics of interest (only applicable for enablers and barriers), answer forms and compulsory-response flags. Treat it as the authoritative field specification. Extract the case-data fields selected by the calling workflow. Use the stable field identifiers and JSON schema supplied by the calling workflow, not improvised identifiers or an output schema inferred from the CSV headings. Do not add any unrequested fields.

General Extraction Rules

1. Apply questions consistently to proposed, current, historical or concluded initiatives. Preserve distinctions in the source's timing and uncertainty.

2. For “Fixed, one option”, select exactly one option permitted by that row's Definition, when supported. For “Fixed, multiple options possible”, return all supported options as an array, without inventing additional categories. Free-text answers should be brief, specific and source-grounded. For fields allowing multiple free-text answers, preserve distinct findings separately.

3. Every CSV field marked Response_compulsory=TRUE must appear in the output for each case. A compulsory field does not authorise a fabricated substantive answer: where information is unavailable, use the schema's explicit missing-value representation. Apply the same evidence and missingness standards to optional fields.

4. Initiation concerns why the initiative arose and what brought or motivated actors to act together. Implementation concerns how it was carried out and what the source says about progress towards its aims. Do not conflate the two.

5. Actors include organisations, companies, financial institutions, government bodies and producers involved in the case. Contributing actors supply resources. Implementing actors carry out the initiative. Name lead actors only where supported. Do not presume that authors, affiliated institutions or funders are participants without evidence of a formal connection.

6. For success-factor and barrier fields, examine the topics in each CSV row's Topics of Interest column, but do not treat those as exhaustive examples or as automatic findings. Describe briefly in your own words (rather than following the format of the list of topics of interest) and by explicitly making the link to the case of collective action the factor. Describe a factor only if the source identifies it explicitly, or by a clearly explained source-grounded inference, to initiation or implementation of the specific case of collective action. Success factors and barriers predating or not affecting the case’s goals or impact on the ground are ineligible. Separate evidence relating to achieving stated objectives from evidence relating to impacts on the ground. Preserve the CSV distinctions among social, legal, design, implementation, environmental and economic factors. Record an explicitly reported absence of influence as “not a factor”, rather than as a positive or negative influence. Allow multiple findings per field.

7. Distinguish actors' own accounts of achievement or failure from independent assessments where the CSV requests this. Do not equate objectives or anticipated benefits with observed impacts, and do not present source assertions as newly verified causal findings.

8. Do not infer geographic location from author affiliations. Use the original source to substantiate actors, commodities, status and geography. Retain contradictions or insufficient information as uncertain rather than resolving them from outside knowledge.

Evidence And Anchors

Use only the supplied full text and its metadata. For every reported substantive answer or distinct finding, provide an exact short, continuous source quotation and its corresponding input anchor, such as [PAGE n], [SECTION n] or [TEXT BLOCK n]. The anchor must occur in the supplied input. Do not invent page numbers for XML or JSON, fabricate quotations, or use external facts. Keep unanswered fields explicitly unreported, with no invented evidence. Preserve a clear linkage between every extracted statement and its citation.

Output Contract

Return ONLY one valid JSON object per input publication, complying exactly with the externally supplied JSON schema (generated by the extraction workflow from the authoritative CSV). It must contain the exact input record_id, an array of distinct cases (possibly empty), and per-case field values and evidence. Each eligible case must have a stable, unique identifier scoped to the publication and an assessment_reason explaining why it meets the case definition. The workflow determines JSON property names, data types, allowed values, array shapes, missing-value encoding and required properties. Do not print a Markdown fence or explanatory text outside JSON. If no eligible case is supported, return an empty cases array with the schema-defined status.
