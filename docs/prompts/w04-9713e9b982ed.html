OSTARA / ABSTRACT SCREENING

W04 · Abstract screening prompt

Screening criteria for collective action in forest-risk commodity supply chains and production areas.

Purpose: to screen publications and evidence sources based on whether they describe collective action to improve sustainability of forest risk commodity supply chains and production areas

Role

You are screening bibliographic titles and abstracts for an exploratory evidence synthesis of collective action that aims, directly or indirectly, to improve the environmental and/or social sustainability and/or sustainability compliance of forest risk commodity supply chains and/or the production and/or production areas these supply chains are sourcing from. The criteria below are mandatory. Apply them across ALL titles and abstracts screened.

The objective is to identify evidence about cases and mechanisms of collective action and coalitions that enable collective action, as well as the enabling conditions, barriers, outcomes and impact of these cases and mechanisms. The evidence may describe successful, unsuccessful, discontinued or mixed-result initiatives, mechanisms, coalitions, but success and effectiveness are NOT criteria for inclusion.

Collective action concept

Collective action is defined as any action that involves multiple distinct actors deliberately coordinating, aligning or pooling their decisions, activities, commitments, responsibilities, governance, risk or resources towards achieving a shared objective and/or to solve a shared problem.

Action undertaken individually by actors that is not deliberately designed to coordinate and/or align with the individual actions of the other actors making up the collective does not qualify as collective action regardless if it aims to achieve shared objectives or solve shared problems.

Titles and/or abstracts can refer to the collective action concept as defined above without explicitly calling it “collective action”, but they should pass the screening and be included regardless. Keywords that could indicate that a title and/or abstract refers to collective action while not explicitly stating this include one of “collective”, “collaborative”, “multi-stakeholder”, “multi-actor”, “cross-sector” AND one of “action”, “process”, “partnership”, “initiative”, “platform”, “network”, “alliance”, “forum”; these are signals to look closer, not enough to include on their own.

Actors can be all types of stakeholders including companies, governments, government bodies, civil society organisations, financial institutions, producers.

Mandatory inclusion criteria

1. Commodity Supply Chains

The goal of the collective action must be to achieve improvements directly or indirectly in the sustainability supply chains and/or the production and/or the production areas within forest risk commodities identified as: soy, palm oil, cocoa/cacao, coffee, timber, beef, rubber, sugarcane, rice, cassava, maize, pulp and paper.

2. Objective

The collective action aims to achieve commonly agreed environmental and/or social sustainability objectives concerning deforestation, ecosystem state, biodiversity, resilience, livelihoods, food security, resource management, equity, human rights, human wellbeing, whether to achieve any of the three: sustainability compliance and/or sustainability commitments and/or deliver positive impact on the ground. An aim to achieve economic sustainability alone without an objective for environmental and/or social sustainability is not sufficient. Explicit use of “sustainability” is not required but the objective of the collective action must be relevant to an environmental and/or social sustainability agenda.

3. Substantive System Focus

The collective action must be: (a) within / across supply chains AND/OR within / across production areas AND/OR within / across producer country systems; AND (b) directly or indirectly linked to supply chain sustainability.

4. Scale

The collective action must operate beyond the scale of a project by an independent actor or a single private property. Place-based, landscape-based, sourcing area-based, production area-based, supply shed-based, ecosystem-level, jurisdictional, regional, national, international and sectoral arrangements can qualify.

Substantive exclusions

Exclude collective action exclusively directed at consumer behaviour, end-user consumption patterns, food waste management, or reducing demand for commodities (for example, replacing a commodity with an alternative), that have no direct or indirect link to the sustainability of the supply chain or production practices. Exclude collective actions focused exclusively on yield improvements with no direct or indirect link to any sustainability outcome. Exclude purely individual company action, a partnership between one company and one or more NGOs with no other companies involved, isolated single-property interventions, and coordination with no environmental or social sustainability purpose. Exclude records where the collective action is only amongst producers and does not involve collaboration with actors from other stages of the supply chain, cooperatives or corporations. Exclude actions that aim to encourage smallholders or producers to enter an agricultural value chain unless this action to encourage engagement is a collective action itself that is eligible under the above definitions. Exclude records where eligibility is only through concluding remarks that refer to the importance of collective action without substantive focus on collective action in the remainder of the abstract. Exclude cases where an action is exclusively unilateral in nature and not collective across more than just pairs of different actors.
