OSTARA / ABSTRACT SCREENING

W08 · Abstract-level case detection

Candidate case detection from bibliographic titles, abstracts and keywords.

Purpose

Identify distinct cases of collective action within forest risk commodity supply chains and/or production areas to deliver improvements to environmental and/or social sustainability from a publication’s title, abstract and keywords. This is candidate detection for targeted full-text verification, not mechanism coding, outcome evaluation, or proof that a case exists.

Definitions

A collective action case [hereafter referred to as ‘case’] is synonymous to a collective action initiative, and is defined as a distinguishable real-world programme or project that brings together multiple actors to collaboratively address shared problems and/or achieve shared objectives. Cases relate to a specific implementation context, which could be a specific place or region, a specific supply chain and commodity, a specific country or market or specific policy setting.

A case qualifies even if it does not explicitly call itself “collective action” if it conforms to the definition of a case set above.

Each case can implement one or more mechanisms of collective action.

Do not mistake a research project, a single commercial service, generic recommendation, or a named location for a case of collective action. Retain multiple distinct cases per publication where applicable. Record and retain multiple distinct cases implemented by the same coalition where applicable i.e. when the same group of actors that act collectively give rise and carry out several distinct real-world initiatives/cases. Do not merge cases merely because they are implemented by the same coalition.

Important Boundaries

  • A named organisation, business, cooperative, project, programme, research study, country or campaign is NOT automatically a collective-action case.
  • Distinguish the arrangement being studied from researchers' data collection, research partnerships, knowledge-transfer activities or the organisation carrying out the study.
  • Generic social mobilisation, an aspiration to collaborate, or an unexplained named initiative is not sufficient.
  • A project or programme is eligible only if the supplied text describes a distinguishable real-world arrangement for coordinated collective action, rather than merely research activities, a technology, a general recommendation or a hypothetical initiative. An explicitly proposed initiative may be retained as a candidate where its actors, intended activities and implementation context are sufficiently identifiable, but must be labelled proposed.
  • A single study can describe multiple genuinely distinct cases. Do not split components of one case into separate cases without evidence of distinct arrangements.
  • Do not infer achievements or effectiveness from objectives, programme membership, or intentions.
  • Never guess named actors, geography, initiative name, commodity or claimed outcomes. Never treat authors' affiliations as a case location.
  • Cite a brief EXACT quotation from the supplied title/abstract as supporting evidence. Do not invent verbatim text.
  • Keep two confidence judgments separate: (1) confidence that a distinguishable case exists, and (2) confidence that it describes collective action for sustainability of forest risk-commodity supply chain and/or production area.
  • Where a potentially distinguishable case is described but its identity, boundaries, coordinated collective action or sustainability purpose cannot be established confidently from the supplied text, classify it as unclear. Preserve the candidate for full-text verification where sufficient identifying information is available.
  • This is publication-level screening with a repeatable array of case candidates, NOT cross-publication deduplication.

Classification

specific_case
Exactly one distinguishable case with sufficiently supported collective sustainability action.
multiple_cases
Two or more distinguishable cases with sufficiently supported collective sustainability action.
no_identifiable_case
No distinguishable case or plausible case candidate identifiable from the supplied text.
unclear
One or more potentially distinguishable cases requiring full-text verification because relevant information is insufficient.

Full-Text Priority

high
Specific relevant cases, or particularly informative ambiguous candidates, suitable for targeted full-text verification.
medium
Plausible candidates with less clearly supported evidence.
low
No identifiable case evidence or insufficiently informative material.

Priority reflects usefulness for verification, not success or effectiveness.

Output

Return exactly one valid JSON object per publication, with no surrounding prose:

{"record_id": "exact supplied ID",
  "case_evidence_status": "specific_case | multiple_cases | no_identifiable_case | unclear",
  "cases": [
    {
      "case_name": null,
      "case_description": "short distinguishing description",
      "case_location": null,
      "case_sector": null,
      "implementation_status": "implemented | proposed | historical | unclear",
      "supporting_evidence": "short exact quotation from supplied title or abstract",
      "confidence": "high | medium | low",
      "collective_action_confidence": "high | medium | low",
      "collective_action_basis": "evidence for coordinated collective action and sustainability purpose, including any uncertainty",
      "case_kind": "collective_arrangement | research_activity | commercial_service | social_mobilisation | other_unclear"
    }
  ],
  "fulltext_priority": "high | medium | low",
  "assessment_reason": "short evidence-based explanation"
}

Consistency Rules

  • specific_case requires exactly one supported case.
  • multiple_cases requires at least two supported cases.
  • no_identifiable_case requires an empty cases array.
  • unclear permits one or more uncertain case candidates, or an empty array where the publication suggests a possible case but does not identify it adequately.
  • Do not classify an uncertain candidate as a confirmed case.
  • case_name, case_location and case_sector may be null.
  • Every reported case must have a case_description and supporting_evidence grounded in the supplied text.
  • Do not assign mechanism ontology codes or assess causal effectiveness.
